What the partner brief changes for the website

Four edits the new positioning forces.

The current site already says "growth operating partner", "Monthly Growth System", "embedded senior operators". Most of that survives. What the partner brief locks in — and what the Brand Guide v2 now enforces — is the four edits below.

01 · Category

Growth Operating System, not Growth Operating Partner.

"Partner" frames us as a relationship type. "Operating System" frames us as a category of one. The hero, nav, and H1s all shift.

02 · Plans

Two streams, one contract.

Current site implies one retainer. New plans show subscription (fixed) + credits (the work, via ALC Menu) as two visible streams.

03 · Playbook

Start-of-cycle commitment, not end-of-cycle report.

Reframes the monthly ritual: Day 1 sign-off, Day 28 Compound Score. The system learns your numbers between.

04 · Strategist

Generic Strategist, not Brand Strategist.

Named as the judgement layer on top of the system. Human face of the OS. Holds the client relationship across plans.

Hero copy · current vs proposed

Current (gritfuel.works)

"A growth operating partner built to embed senior operators, align monthly priorities, and compound performance over time."

Correct but buried. Reads like a consultancy one-pager. No category claim, no product.

Proposed

"The growth operating system for founders ready to scale."

Category claim up-front. Specific buyer. Pairs with the umbrella line "Human thinking. Relentless execution." as eyebrow. Lead line does the work the old H1 failed to do.

Plans block · current vs proposed

Current

Single all-in monthly price per tier.

Implies retainer. Can't show what the money actually buys. Credits & ALC Menu are invisible to buyers.

Proposed

Two visible streams: Subscription (€3,500–€8,000) + Credits (€5,000–€12,000+).

Shows the model. The subscription buys the OS (Dashboard, Playbook, Strategist, cadence). Credits buy the deliverables from the ALC Menu. "No mystery retainer."

My read

Go with B.
Keep A as the fallback.
Steal two sections from C.

B is the best-payoff-per-day option. It lands all four positioning shifts (category, plans, playbook, strategist) with a structure buyers can skim in 30 seconds. A is too thin — it solves the plans problem but leaves the Growth OS story invisible. C is a brand exercise more than a site exercise; for a minimal refinement, it's over-scope.

Next steps

1. Pick a direction. If we hold the 29 April go-live, A is the safe play. If we can shift go-live to mid-May, B is the right call. Decision needed this week.

2. Source the imagery. Two illustrations from B/C have no source yet: the Monthly Playbook cover (we have the real file — just needs art direction) and the strategist portrait. Both are blockers on final visual polish.

3. Validate with partners. Drop this comparison link into the WhatsApp thread with Tlali/Micha/Kim. Ask for A/B/C votes — not copy edits yet.

4. Steal from C. Regardless of A vs B, the editorial step-rail (01/02/03 large numerals) and the lime Dashboard band from C are stronger than B's versions and should be lifted.
